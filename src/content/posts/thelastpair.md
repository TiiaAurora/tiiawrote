## The Last Pair
<br>
There is a certain sadness in outgrowing a space.<br>
Growth is silent and you won’t notice it <br>
until you hit the inner shell of your reality<br>
Like when your toes hit the tip of your shoe as a child <br>
when it was time to get a new pair <br>
But as a child nobody questions growth<br>
Your parents buy a new pair, you chose the color, maybe the motive<br>
And for the next few months life has space again <br>
You are not limited anymore, your toes have wiggle room.
<br><br>
As an adult, growth is painful, because nobody expects it.<br>
In fact it is heavily discouraged because once you outgrow those<br>
who want to keep you contained, you make an enemy. <br>
Because it is easier to stop you than to keep up with you. <br>
So growth becomes lonely, because life is a spectrum of trauma bonding. <br>
And when you choose to heal, <br>
you forget your mother tongue.
<br><br>
You connect with cities, you feel they are like a mold around your soul.<br>
Then your toes hit reality again and you move on. <br>
You meet people that you call friends, maybe even love. <br>
Those feel like the people who get you.<br>
Soon the world they curated for their own stability suffocates you.<br>
And you create cracks by asking questions <br>
And you move on, because you have to. 
<br><br>
And the more often you move on, the less people you meet. <br>
But the people you do meet are extraordinary. <br>
Looking back on your path you see versions of yourself left behind<br>
Like outgrown shoes in the memories of those you called friends. <br>
There is no real end to this journey.<br>
There is only the choice of how lonely is too lonely. <br>
And which person is so important to you <br>
that you do not want to leave them behind.
<br><br>
